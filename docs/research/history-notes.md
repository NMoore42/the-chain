# Fleetwood Mac / Stevie Nicks — Verified History Notes

Fact-check reference for a fan-made, non-commercial word game. Compiled 31 Oct 2026.

**Conventions**
- Every entry carries a source URL that was fetched or that surfaced from a reliable domain during research (Wikipedia and its cited sources, officialcharts.com, Grammy.com, RIAA.com, rockhall.com, Rolling Stone, Billboard, NPR, CBS News).
- **UNVERIFIED** = no reliable source found. **DISPUTED** = reliable sources disagree; competing values are listed.
- Billboard.com, RollingStone.com and AllMusic article bodies are paywalled or blocked to automated fetching; where cited, the headline/date/snippet was visible in search results and the fact was cross-checked elsewhere.
- No song lyrics appear in this file. Song titles only.
- Chart peaks are US Billboard (Hot 100 / Billboard 200) unless marked UK (Official Charts).

## Scrapbook Cards — Verified Behind-the-Scenes Facts

1. **The name came before the band.** Peter Green named a 1967 instrumental "Fleetwood Mac" after his favourite rhythm section — drummer Mick Fleetwood and bassist John McVie — then used the name to persuade McVie to join. Fleetwood and McVie are the only members on every studio album. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac_(1968_album) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Fleetwood
2. **The penguin mascot is John McVie's doing.** Living near London Zoo early in his marriage, he joined the Zoological Society and spent hours watching the penguins; the bird became the band emblem and the title of the 1973 album *Penguin*. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_(album)
3. **Christine drew the Kiln House cover** while still an uncredited guest on the record (contract with Blue Horizon); she joined officially days later. The house was two converted hop-drying oast houses in Hampshire where the band lived together in 1970 — Mick married Jenny Boyd there. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiln_House
4. **Mick's wooden balls are toilet-chain pulls.** By his own account they began as lavatory chains hung from his waist as a cheeky blues joke in the band's early club days; they became a good-luck charm, appear on the *Rumours* cover, and the cover pair sold at auction for $128,000. — https://americansongwriter.com/rumours-fleetwood-mac-album-cover-story/ ; https://www.musicradar.com/news/fleetwood-mac-auction-wooden-balls ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumours_(album)
5. **"Rhiannon" came from a paperback.** Stevie wrote it in 1973 after reading Mary Leader's novel *Triad*; she only learned of the Welsh legend of Rhiannon in 1978. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhiannon_(song)
6. **Stevie was Keith Olsen's cleaning lady.** Before joining, she worked as a restaurant hostess, a waitress and housekeeper for the producer who would introduce her music to Mick Fleetwood. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Nicks ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Olsen
7. **A package deal on New Year's Eve.** Fleetwood phoned Olsen on 31 Dec 1974 asking for the guitarist from "Frozen Love"; Lindsey said he and Stevie came together. Both were hired without an audition. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac_(1975_album)
8. **"The Chain" was literally spliced together.** It began as Christine's "Keep Me There", borrowed Lindsey's intro from a 1973 Buckingham Nicks track, took Stevie's lyrics from a different song, and got its bass outro from John and Mick mid-take — all cut together with razor blades. It is the only song credited to all five members, and the BBC's Formula 1 theme. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chain
9. **"Songbird" was recorded in an empty concert hall.** Christine sang it alone at Zellerbach Auditorium, Berkeley, overnight on 3–4 Mar 1976, with flowers and spotlights set up to feel like a show. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songbird_(Fleetwood_Mac_song)
10. **Everyone in bed for Rolling Stone.** Annie Leibovitz shot all five members in one bed for the 24 Mar 1977 cover, as the band's relationships were coming apart. — https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/rock-list-favorite-rolling-stone-covers-215797/
11. **"Dreams" was written in Sly Stone's studio.** Stevie wrote the band's only US #1 single in early 1976 in a room at the Record Plant said to belong to Sly Stone. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_(Fleetwood_Mac_song)
12. **A marching band in an empty stadium.** The USC Trojan Marching Band recorded "Tusk" at Dodger Stadium on 4 Jun 1979 while the Dodgers were away; John McVie, absent, was represented by a cardboard cutout. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusk_(song)
13. **Prince played on "Stand Back" for free (sort of).** Stevie wrote it on her wedding day in 1983 while "Little Red Corvette" played on the radio; Prince came to the studio, played uncredited synth, and took half the publishing. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Back
14. **"Edge of Seventeen" is a mishearing.** Tom Petty's wife Jane said she met Tom at the "age of seventeen"; Stevie heard "edge" through her accent and kept it. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_Seventeen
15. **A fake Fleetwood Mac went on tour.** In Jan 1974 the band's manager sent a different group out under the name; the tour collapsed within weeks and the real band sat out nearly a year. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Davis_(music_manager)
16. **Lindsey's last tour for 15 years was filmed.** The Mirage Tour shows at the LA Forum, 21–22 Oct 1982, were the last with Buckingham until The Dance in 1997. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_Tour
17. **A skateboard brought "Dreams" back.** Nathan Apodaca's 25 Sep 2020 TikTok sent "Dreams" back onto the Hot 100 for the first time since 1977; Mick and Stevie posted their own replies. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_(Fleetwood_Mac_song)

## Corrections Log (claims in the brief that were wrong, imprecise or unverifiable)

- "Man of the World" peaked UK #2, not #1; "Oh Well" UK #2 / US #55.
- *Then Play On* UK peak is #6 (Official Charts), not #4.
- "Albatross" was released 22 Nov 1968; its single week at UK #1 was the chart dated 29 Jan 1969.
- Peter Green's last show date is DISPUTED (20 vs 28 May 1970); venue unverified.
- Boston Tea Party dates: 5–7 Feb 1970. Warehouse New Orleans: 30 Jan–1 Feb 1970 (venue's opening night). Fillmore and Marquee dates unverified.
- The "Fleetwood Mac" instrumental was recorded 19 Apr 1967 at Decca and is not on *A Hard Road*.
- Christine officially joined 1 Aug 1970 (before *Kiln House* came out, 18 Sep 1970). She *drew* the cover (Wikipedia's wording), rather than "painted".
- McVie wedding exact date unverified (year 1968 only).
- Bob Welch "April 1971" unverified — sources say spring/summer 1971.
- Weston and Walker joined Sep 1972. *Penguin* release disputed Jan vs Mar 1973.
- The 1973 tour was abandoned after 20 Oct 1973 (Lincoln, NE) rather than cancelled in advance; fake-Mac lawsuit outcome is disputed between sources.
- Buckingham/Nicks joined New Year's Eve 1974; "officially Jan 1975" is just when recording began.
- *Fleetwood Mac* (1975) reached #1 on 4 Sep 1976; "58 weeks" is its 58th chart week (57 after debut).
- "Rhiannon" and "Say You Love Me" peaked #11, "Over My Head" #20.
- "Landslide" year disputed (1973 vs 1974).
- Rumours studios: add Producer's Workshop, Sound City and Davlen; "Songbird" was 3–4 Mar 1976.
- *Rumours* is RIAA 21x Platinum (2023), not 20x.
- No Wembley 1977 show found — London dates were the Rainbow Theatre, 8–10 Apr 1977. Forum shows were 29–31 Aug 1977; Day on the Green was 7 May 1977.
- *Tusk* cost reported as $1–1.4 million. *Mirage* released 2 Jul 1982, not June. Mirage video title is *In Concert – Mirage Tour '82* (release year disputed 1983/84).
- Buckingham quit 8 Aug 1987 after a 7 Aug meeting; Burnette and Vito joined Sep 1987.
- *Behind the Mask*: UK #1, US #18; Nicks and Christine announced their last *tour*, not departure (Nicks left 1991).
- Mick's wooden balls originate as lavatory chains; "African talking drum" prop unverified.
- *Bella Donna* label: Modern/Atco; hit #1 on chart dated 5 Sep 1981.
- "Leather and Lace" was written by Nicks alone. "I Can't Wait" US release Feb 1986.
- Maxwellington the cockatoo is documented on the Rolling Stone cover, not confirmed on the album cover.
- Stevie has never won a solo Grammy (8 nominations). Top hat origin unverified.
- Rock Hall 1998: Waldorf-Astoria; presenter Sheryl Crow; eight inductees (Welch excluded); band played "Landslide", "Big Love", "Say You Love Me".
- No Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award for Fleetwood Mac. Stevie Nicks was NOT MusiCares Person of the Year 2024 (Jon Bon Jovi was); Fleetwood Mac was the 2018 honoree.
- Sales: 120 million is the claimed figure; 103.7 million certified.
